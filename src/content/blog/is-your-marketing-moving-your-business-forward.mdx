Marketing never stops. Websites need updating, social media posts need creating and scheduling, PPC and email campaigns need attention, blogs need writing, you need to stay on top of industry trends — the list goes on.

There is always plenty of activity to report on, but a full marketing calendar can still leave an important question unanswered: what is all of this activity helping the business achieve?

For organisations in addiction, mental health, behavioural health and wellness, that question needs careful consideration. Marketing must communicate complex services, reflect professional expertise, and reach people who may be in crisis, and whose decisions may involve several conversations and considerable thought.

Visibility is useful, but it needs a purpose, which means being clear about what the business needs its marketing to achieve and whether the work being delivered contributes to those goals.

## Being busy does not always mean making progress

Marketing can keep a team busy without moving the business forward. Posts are published, campaigns are running and the monthly report is full of charts. There is plenty to show for the work, but how much of it is working together to achieve something useful?

Disconnected activity is part of the problem. Social media promotes one message, advertising leads with another, and the website does not quite support either. Each piece may look good on its own, but the audience is left to join the dots. More activity can simply add to the confusion.

The same applies to reporting. Impressions, likes and follower counts can produce attractive graphs, but they become vanity metrics when there is no explanation of their relevance to the business. A rising number tells you something has increased. It does not, on its own, tell you that your marketing is making progress.

Reach and engagement can be useful indicators, but they need context. Who are you reaching? What are they responding to? Does that attention support the services, relationships or professional recognition the organisation is trying to develop? Without that connection, the report gives you pretty pictures and very little basis for deciding what to do next.

Productive marketing has a purpose that carries through the work. The activity supports a consistent message, and the reporting helps the business understand its contribution. That is what makes it possible to judge whether the time, effort and budget being invested are worthwhile.

## What is the marketing activity actually delivering?

“Growing the business” is always the ultimate aim, but it leaves considerable room for interpretation, and without direction, work gets completed without anyone being clear about whether it supports the organisation’s most important priorities.

Marketing activity can build around requests and habits, and if the top-level plan isn’t cohesive, it can lead to busyness for everyone with no real business progress. Marketing ends up responding to whoever needs something next or whoever shouts the loudest. Each request may be reasonable, but collectively they can pull the team in different directions.

Every department will have priorities that influence what the organisation communicates, whom it needs to reach and what progress would look like. But has this been agreed collectively, and does the marketing team know which outcomes matter most, or is everyone working towards their own interpretation of growth?

If stronger referral relationships are a priority, the work commissioned and delivered should reflect that. If the business needs more suitable enquiries for a particular service, the marketing should support that objective. Otherwise, how can anyone judge whether marketing is moving the business forward?

Meeting a department’s request completes a task. Whether it contributes to the overall business objective is a different story.

## Individual activities need to support each other

If stronger referral relationships are a priority, the work commissioned and delivered should reflect that. If the business needs more suitable enquiries for a particular service, the marketing should support that objective. The connection needs to be understood by both the people requesting the work and those responsible for delivering it.

That connection also needs to carry through the individual activities. An advert may introduce a service that is difficult to find on the website. Social posts may emphasise an area of expertise that barely appears in the organisation’s main messaging. A professional recommendation may lead someone to an outdated biography or a page that leaves basic questions unanswered.

Each piece of work may have been completed to a good standard, but together they present a fragmented picture. People have to search for missing information or make sense of different descriptions before they can decide whether to enquire.

For example, a provider launching a specialist programme may run a campaign that attracts relevant interest. But if visitors arrive at a general website page that does not explain the programme, the marketing has brought them to a point where their questions remain unanswered.

The website, advertising, written content and professional materials each have a role to play. They need to support the same business priority and give people a consistent understanding of what the organisation offers. Looking at each activity separately can miss the gaps between them, even when individual channel reports suggest things are going well.

## Progress requires context

A marketing report can show you metrics that demonstrate that there is marketing activity, but it doesn’t necessarily explain how that contributes to the business goals and objectives.

The numbers need context. A busy month and a successful month can look similar in a report, even when they mean very different things for the organisation. More enquiries are not necessarily progress if they come from people looking for services you do not offer. Equally, an article with fewer readers may be valuable if it reaches referral professionals who need to understand your expertise.

What counts as progress also depends on the agreed priority. For a treatment provider, enquiry suitability matters alongside volume. For an expert building professional recognition, relevant invitations and stronger relationships may matter more than an immediate increase in service enquiries.

Time needs to be considered too. Some activity supports an immediate campaign; other work builds familiarity, understanding and professional relationships over a longer period. Every post, email or article does not need to produce an immediate enquiry, but its contribution should still be understood. Longer-term goals should not become a reason to keep investing in work without reviewing whether it is helping.

Reporting should give the business enough insight to decide what to continue, change or reconsider. If the conversation stops at how much was published or how many people saw it, the original question remains unanswered: what is all of this activity helping the business achieve?

## Clear direction makes delivery more valuable

Marketing gains purpose when the organisation can connect its priorities to the work being delivered.

That connection does not remove uncertainty. Audience decisions are influenced by circumstances beyond a campaign, and even well-considered activity may need refinement. It does, however, make those decisions easier to assess.

The organisation has a clearer understanding of whom it needs to reach, why a particular activity matters and how the different parts of its marketing support one another. It can discuss progress with greater confidence and make choices based on relevance rather than pressure to keep doing more.

At The Square Peg Agency, we believe understanding the business is essential to delivering useful marketing. For organisations working in addiction, mental health, behavioural health and wellness, that means respecting the complexity of their services while maintaining a clear commercial purpose.

The question is worth returning to as the business evolves: is your marketing helping you move towards the opportunities and outcomes that matter to you?
